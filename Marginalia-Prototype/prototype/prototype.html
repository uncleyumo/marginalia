What a Pointer Really Is

Tap any word for its sound and its meaning

Most beginners meet pointers in the second week of C, and most beginners hate them. That is fair.

A pointer is just a variable that stores an address. An address is a number that says where a value lives in memory.

When you write int x = 5;, the compiler reserves a small block of memory and puts 5 inside it.

To read the value, you dereference it: *p gives you 5.

If you dereference a pointer that was never set, your program crashes with a segmentation fault.

You can also allocate memory yourself. malloc asks the heap for a block. Forget free and the memory leaks.
